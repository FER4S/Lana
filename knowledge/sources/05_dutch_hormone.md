## DUTCH Test Hormonal Cascades & Pattern Interpretation

Practitioner handout for functional dietitian handover & review.

Key hormonal systems in DUTCH testing:

- HPA Axis (Cortisol & Cortisone Pattern)
- Sex Hormones (Estrogen, Progesterone, Testosterone)
- Androgen Pathways (DHEA, Testosterone, 5α/5β Pathways)
- Estrogen Metabolism (Phase I & II Detox)
- Melatonin & Neurotransmitters (optional panel)

### 1. HPA Axis: Cortisol & Cortisone

**Markers:** Free cortisol (diurnal rhythm), metabolized cortisol, cortisone (inactive form).

**Pattern Recognition:**

| Pattern | Meaning |
| :-: | :-: |
| Low Free + Low Metabolized | Adrenal insufficiency, HPA axis suppression (chronic stress, burnout, steroid use) |
| High Free + Low Metabolized | Acute stress, poor clearance (liver dysfunction, thyroid issues) |
| High Free + High Metabolized | HPA activation (early stress response, inflammation, insulin resistance) |
| Low Free + High Metabolized | Over-clearance (hypermetabolic state, T3 dominance, liver overactivity) |

**Check:** Diurnal curve. Should peak in the morning and gradually decline. Flat or reversed curves suggest dysfunction.

### 2. Estrogen & Progesterone Cascade

**Markers:** Estrone (E1), Estradiol (E2), Estriol (E3); Progesterone (measured as α-Pregnanediol + β-Pregnanediol).

**Pattern Recognition:**

| Pattern | Clinical Insight |
| :-: | :-: |
| Low E2 + Low Progesterone | Ovarian insufficiency, post-menopause, HPA suppression |
| High E2 + Low Progesterone | Estrogen dominance, PMS, fibroids, breast tenderness |
| High E2 + High Progesterone | Luteal phase (if timed correctly), fertility support |
| Low E2 + High Progesterone | Rare—may be due to exogenous hormones or abnormal metabolism |

**Ratio matters:** E2:P (optimal 100–300:1 urine ratio)

### 3. Testosterone & Androgens

**Markers:** DHEA-S, Testosterone, 5α-DHT, 5β-androstanediol.

**Pathway Flow:** DHEA → Androstenedione → Testosterone → DHT (via 5α-reductase)

**Pattern Recognition:**

| Pattern | Interpretation |
| :-: | :-: |
| Low DHEA/Testosterone | HPA suppression, aging, hypogonadism |
| High DHEA/Testosterone | PCOS, insulin resistance, adrenal hyperactivity |
| High 5α-DHT | 5α-dominance (acne, hirsutism, prostate issues) |
| High 5β Pathway | Often linked with inflammation, fatigue |

**5α:5β Ratio:** High = androgenic symptoms; Low = fatigue/chronic illness.

### 4. Estrogen Metabolism: Detox Pathways

**Phase I (Hydroxylation)**

- 2-OH-E1 (protective)
- 4-OH-E1 (DNA-damaging, carcinogenic)
- 16-OH-E1 (estrogenic, proliferative)

**Preferred Pathway:** 2-OH > 4-OH and 16-OH

| Risk Factor | Interpretation |
| :-: | :-: |
| High 4-OH | DNA damage potential (requires methylation support) |
| High 16-OH | Estrogen dominance symptoms (weight gain, fibroids) |

**Phase II (Methylation)**

Me-2-OH-E1 = Methylated protective form of estrogen

Low methylation ratio (Me-2-OH/2-OH) → Poor COMT activity → Consider magnesium, B12, methyl donors.

### 5. Melatonin & Neurohormones (if panel included)

**Marker:** 6-OH-melatonin

Low: Poor sleep quality, pineal gland suppression, blue light exposure

Use alongside cortisol rhythm for circadian insights.

### Clinical Pattern Flags

| Symptom | Pattern |
| :-: | :-: |
| Fatigue, burnout | Low free & metabolized cortisol |
| Anxiety, wired but tired | High free cortisol, low progesterone |
| PMS, painful periods | High estrogen, low progesterone |
| Acne, oily skin, hair loss | 5α dominance, high DHEA/testosterone |
| Infertility, irregular periods | Low progesterone, estrogen imbalance |

### Action Plan During Review

- Review timing: Ensure test was done during correct cycle day (Days 19–21 for cycling women).
- Correlate with symptoms: Use symptom mapping against hormone patterns.
- Look for root drivers: Stress, liver detox capacity, inflammation, blood sugar, thyroid.
- Refer or collaborate: Identify where deeper work is needed (e.g., methylation, GI detox, trauma).

Nutritional support examples:

| Imbalance | Nutritional Support |
| :-: | :-: |
| Low cortisol | B5, C, licorice, adaptogens (morning) |
| Estrogen dominance | DIM, crucifers, fiber, liver detox |
| Low progesterone | Vit C, E, B6, chasteberry |
| Poor methylation | B12, folate, Mg, SAMe |
| High DHEA/testosterone | Saw palmetto, spearmint tea, insulin support |

## DUTCH Test Findings: Functional Interpretation & Interventions

### SECTION 1: CORTISOL DYSREGULATION

**A. High Free Cortisol or Cortisone**
→ Often seen with perceived stress, inflammation, early-stage HPA axis dysfunction, or poor detox.
Look for: High cortisol throughout the day, high cortisone metabolites.

Intervention Strategies:

- Reduce stimulant intake (caffeine, alcohol, nicotine).
- Support liver detox (e.g., cruciferous vegetables, NAC, glutathione).
- Adaptogens: Rhodiola, holy basil, ashwagandha (if not contraindicated).
- Improve sleep hygiene and blood sugar regulation.

**B. Low Free Cortisol (with or without low total cortisol metabolites)**
→ May indicate HPA axis suppression, chronic stress burnout, or nutrient deficiencies.
Look for: Flattened cortisol curve, low morning output.

Intervention Strategies:

- Emphasize morning sunlight, consistent wake time.
- Phosphatidylserine, vitamin C, B5, and magnesium.
- Use adrenal tonics (e.g., licorice root — if blood pressure permits).
- Address blood sugar crashes and hidden infections.

**C. Cortisol Awakening Response (CAR) Imbalance**
→ A blunted CAR suggests burnout or depression. An elevated CAR may indicate anticipatory anxiety.

Intervention Strategies:

- Morning mindfulness, grounding practices, light exposure.
- Protein-rich breakfast within 1 hour of waking.
- Consider mitochondrial support (CoQ10, B vitamins, L-carnitine).

### SECTION 2: ESTROGEN ISSUES

**A. Estrogen Dominance (High Estradiol or Poor Estrogen Metabolism)**
→ Can contribute to PMS, breast tenderness, heavy periods, mood swings, fibroids.
Look for: Elevated E1/E2, high E3, low 2-OH:16-OH ratio, slow COMT activity.

Intervention Strategies:

- Fiber (25–35g/day), DIM/I3C, calcium d-glucarate.
- Reduce xenoestrogens (plastics, skincare products).
- Support liver Phase I/II detoxification pathways.

**B. Low Estrogen**
→ Associated with fatigue, vaginal dryness, mood issues, low libido.
Look for: Low E1/E2, especially postmenopausal or after restrictive diets.

Intervention Strategies:

- Phytoestrogens (flaxseed, fermented soy, red clover).
- Nutritional support: omega-3s, B vitamins, vitamin E.
- Consider hormone replacement therapy referral if indicated.

### SECTION 3: PROGESTERONE IMBALANCE

**A. Low Progesterone**
→ Symptoms include anxiety, short luteal phase, insomnia, PMS.
Look for: Low pregnenolone, low progesterone/estradiol ratio.

Intervention Strategies:

- Stress reduction and HPA axis support.
- Vitex agnus-castus (chasteberry).
- Magnesium, B6, and zinc.
- Consider luteal phase progesterone creams (if appropriate).

### SECTION 4: ANDROGEN IMBALANCE

**A. High DHEA/Testosterone**
→ Often PCOS-related or stress-driven. Symptoms: acne, hirsutism, aggression.

Intervention Strategies:

- Reduce insulin resistance: inositol, chromium, low-glycemic diet.
- Zinc, spearmint tea, N-acetyl cysteine (NAC).
- Address gut microbiome (e.g., dysbiosis or β-glucuronidase activity).

**B. Low DHEA/Testosterone**
→ Symptoms: low motivation, libido, poor muscle tone.

Intervention Strategies:

- DHEA precursors (B5, magnesium, vitamin C).
- Adaptogenic herbs, strength training, quality sleep.

### SECTION 5: MELATONIN (6-OHMS)

**A. Low Melatonin**
→ Poor sleep quality, immune dysfunction, increased oxidative stress.

Intervention Strategies:

- Encourage darkness at night, light in the morning.
- Supplemental melatonin (0.3–3 mg), tart cherry juice, magnesium glycinate.

### SECTION 6: ORGANIC ACIDS

Common Imbalances:

- Low B6: mood swings, estrogen metabolism issues.
- Low B12 or methylation support: fatigue, poor detox.
- High oxidative stress markers: prioritize antioxidant support (vitamin C, NAC, glutathione).

### Practitioner Notes

- Always correlate DUTCH results with clinical presentation and symptoms.
- Use DUTCH trends to guide personalized nutrition, lifestyle, and targeted supplementation.
- Re-test only after 3–6 months of intervention unless clinically indicated sooner.

## Summary

### SECTION 1: DUTCH TEST OVERVIEW

What the DUTCH Test Measures:

- Sex Hormones: Estradiol (E2), Estrone (E1), Estriol (E3), Progesterone (a-pregnanediol, b-pregnanediol), Testosterone (T), DHEA
- Cortisol & Cortisone: Free and metabolized levels across 4 points
- Melatonin (6-OH-Melatonin Sulfate)
- Organic Acids: B12 (methylmalonic acid), B6, glutathione, dopamine, norepinephrine, serotonin metabolites
- Hormone Metabolites: Estrogen pathways (2-OH, 4-OH, 16-OH), androgen and progesterone breakdown

### SECTION 2: INTERPRETING KEY PATTERNS

**ESTROGEN DOMINANCE**

Clues:

- High estradiol with low progesterone
- Elevated 16-OH-E1 or poor 2:16-OH ratio
- PMS, painful/heavy periods, fibroids, weight gain

Supportive Interventions:

- DIM or I3C to favor 2-OH metabolism
- Calcium-D-glucarate to improve phase II clearance
- Magnesium, B6, methylated B vitamins
- Reduce xenoestrogens (plastics, personal care)
- Cruciferous vegetables, fiber, liver support (dandelion, milk thistle)

**LOW PROGESTERONE ("ESTROGEN DOMINANCE" by ratio)**

Clues:

- Low β-pregnanediol and α-pregnanediol
- Short luteal phase, anxiety, insomnia

Supportive Interventions:

- Vitex agnus-castus (chasteberry)
- Magnesium glycinate at night
- Vitamin B6, Vitamin E
- Stress support (cortisol may be suppressing ovulation)

**TESTOSTERONE IMBALANCE**

High Testosterone/DHT:

- Acne, PCOS, hair thinning, irritability
- Elevated T with high 5α-DHT metabolite

Low Testosterone:

- Fatigue, low libido, muscle loss
- Low T with low 5α/5β metabolites

Supportive Interventions:

- Saw palmetto or nettle root (for high T)
- Zinc, reishi mushroom
- DHEA or ashwagandha (for low T)
- Address blood sugar/insulin resistance (key in PCOS)

**ADRENAL DYSREGULATION**

a. Low Free Cortisol + Low Metabolized Cortisol — "Flatlined adrenals," burnout, fatigue, poor resilience

Support:

- Adaptogens: Rhodiola, Eleuthero, Ashwagandha (AM)
- Phosphatidylserine (PM if wired/tired)
- B-complex, vitamin C, sleep hygiene

b. High Free Cortisol + Low Metabolized Cortisol — Acute stress response, poor detox capacity, sluggish liver

Support:

- Calm inflammation (curcumin, omega-3s)
- Support liver detox (glutathione, NAC, crucifers)
- Mind-body therapies (HRV, breathwork)

c. High Metabolized Cortisol + Low Free Cortisol — Overclearance pattern, often seen in hyperthyroid or fast metabolizers

Support:

- Consider thyroid function
- Promote tissue retention: licorice root extract
- Evaluate for inflammation or gut dysfunction

**MELATONIN DEFICIENCY**

- Low 6-OH-melatonin sulfate
- Sleep difficulty, low night cortisol not declining

Support:

- Nighttime magnesium, melatonin (0.3–1 mg)
- Light hygiene: low blue light at night
- Morning sunlight exposure

### SECTION 3: FUNCTIONAL TREATMENT PHASES

**Phase 1: Foundations (2–4 weeks)**

- Clean, anti-inflammatory diet (low sugar, high fiber, cruciferous veg)
- Sleep schedule optimization
- Stress resilience: breathwork, walking, adaptogens
- Basic supplements: magnesium, B-complex, omega-3

**Phase 2: Hormone Clearance & Balance (4–8 weeks)**

- Estrogen detox support (DIM, calcium D-glucarate, liver herbs)
- Progesterone support (Vitex, B6, magnesium)
- Androgen modulation (zinc, saw palmetto, insulin balance)

**Phase 3: HPA Axis Reset (as needed)**

- Morning light, early meals, steady protein
- Licorice root or adaptogens based on pattern
- Cortisol testing follow-up after 3 months

**Phase 4: Retesting & Refinement**

- Retest DUTCH at 3–6 months post-intervention
- Adjust based on progress and symptom tracking

### SECTION 4: COMMON PITFALLS TO AVOID

- Treating hormone levels without understanding metabolite patterns
- Ignoring detox capacity (phase I vs. phase II estrogen clearance)
