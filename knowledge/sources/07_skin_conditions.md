**Overview:** Skin conditions like Acne, Rosacea, and Eczema are common inflammatory disorders with
multifactorial causes including genetics, immune dysregulation, gut health, lifestyle, and
environmental triggers. Functional medicine aims to identify root causes and support acute symptoms
holistically.

## 1. Acne

### Root Causes

- Hormonal imbalances: Androgen excess (especially in PCOS), insulin resistance.
- Excess sebum production: Often linked to hormones and diet.
- Skin microbiome imbalance: Overgrowth of Cutibacterium acnes.
- Chronic inflammation: Linked to systemic inflammation, diet, and stress.
- Gut dysbiosis and permeability: Increased inflammatory markers and immune activation.
- Nutritional deficiencies: Zinc, vitamin A, omega-3 fatty acids.
- Environmental triggers: Pollution, harsh skincare products.

### Functional Root Cause Assessment

- Hormonal panel (androgens, insulin, cortisol)
- GI health (stool test for dysbiosis, SIBO, parasites)
- Inflammatory markers (CRP, food sensitivities)
- Nutrient labs (zinc, vitamin A, D, omega-3)
- Lifestyle factors (sleep, stress, diet high in dairy and refined carbs)

### Acute Support & Management

- Gentle cleansing twice daily with non-comedogenic, pH balanced products.
- Topical botanical antimicrobials (e.g., tea tree oil, aloe vera) to reduce bacteria.
- Zinc supplementation (30-50 mg/day) with food.
- Omega-3 fatty acids (fish oil 1-3g EPA/DHA daily) to reduce inflammation.
- Low glycemic load diet focusing on whole foods.
- Reduce dairy and high-sugar foods.
- Manage stress through mindfulness and sleep hygiene.
- Avoid picking or harsh scrubbing.
- Consider short-term botanical anti-inflammatories or gentle topical retinoids.
- **Refer to dermatologist if severe cystic acne or scarring present.**

## 2. Rosacea

### Root Causes

- Chronic inflammation and vascular dysregulation.
- Dysbiosis including overgrowth of Demodex mites.
- Impaired gut barrier and systemic inflammation.
- UV exposure and oxidative stress.
- Trigger sensitivity (heat, spicy foods, alcohol, stress).
- Nutritional deficiencies: omega-3s, antioxidants.

### Functional Root Cause Assessment

- Skin evaluation (rosacea subtype classification)
- Gut microbiome/stool testing for dysbiosis or SIBO.
- Inflammatory and oxidative stress markers.
- Nutrient status: antioxidants (vitamin C, E, selenium), omega-3.
- Identify triggers via detailed history.

### Acute Support & Management

- Avoid known triggers (sun, spicy food, hot beverages, alcohol).
- Use gentle, fragrance-free, non-irritating skincare.
- Daily broad-spectrum physical sun protection (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide).
- Topical botanical anti-inflammatories (e.g., niacinamide, chamomile).
- Omega-3 supplementation (anti-inflammatory effect).
- Gut support if dysbiosis present: probiotics, prebiotics, antimicrobials if needed.
- Stress management.
- Avoid overuse of steroids on face.
- **Referral to dermatologist for possible laser or topical prescription meds if moderate/severe.**

## 3. Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis)

### Root Causes

- Impaired skin barrier function (filaggrin mutations, dryness).
- Immune dysregulation (Th2 skewed inflammation).
- Allergic triggers (food, airborne allergens, irritants).
- Gut dysbiosis and increased permeability.
- Nutritional deficiencies (vitamin D, essential fatty acids).
- Stress and environmental irritants.

### Functional Root Cause Assessment

- Allergy testing (IgE, skin prick or elimination diets).
- Gut microbiome analysis.
- Nutrient labs (vitamin D, zinc, omega-3).
- Assessment of lifestyle/environmental exposures.
- Evaluate personal care products for irritants.

### Acute Support & Management

- Gentle, fragrance-free moisturizing several times daily (ointments preferred).
- Lukewarm showers, avoid harsh soaps.
- Use emollients containing ceramides or natural oils.
- Avoid known allergens and irritants.
- Topical botanical anti-inflammatories (e.g., calendula, chamomile).
- Supplement vitamin D if low.
- Omega-3 fatty acids for immune modulation.
- Consider probiotics for gut barrier support.
- Stress reduction techniques.
- Acute flare: short-term use of topical corticosteroids or calcineurin inhibitors under guidance.
- **Referral to dermatologist if severe or infected lesions.**

## Practical Tips

- Maintain a consistent gentle skincare routine.
- Keep a symptom and trigger diary.
- Focus on anti-inflammatory, whole foods diet with minimal processed sugars and dairy.
- Stay hydrated.
- Manage stress through mindfulness, sleep, and physical activity.
- Support gut health with probiotics and prebiotics as indicated.
- Work closely with healthcare providers for personalized treatment.

# The Gut-Liver-Skin Axis

## Introduction

Skin health is closely interconnected with the gut and liver function. Imbalances in these systems
can manifest as skin conditions such as acne, eczema, psoriasis, rosacea, and dermatitis. The
Gut-Liver-Skin axis explains this connection:

- **Gut:** Microbiome dysbiosis, increased intestinal permeability ("leaky gut"), and poor digestion lead to systemic inflammation.
- **Liver:** Impaired detoxification and bile flow can increase circulating toxins and inflammatory mediators.
- **Skin:** Acts as a secondary detox organ and reflects internal imbalances via inflammation, eruptions, dryness, or itching.

## Step 1: Comprehensive Assessment

- Skin history: Type, triggers, patterns, associated symptoms (itching, dryness, scaling).
- Gut symptoms: Bloating, constipation, diarrhea, food intolerances, reflux.
- Liver function: Fatigue, abdominal discomfort, jaundice, hormonal imbalance.
- Lifestyle factors: Diet, stress, sleep, skincare products, environmental exposures.

Laboratory tests:

- Gut: Comprehensive stool analysis, SIBO breath test, food sensitivity panel.
- Liver: Liver enzymes (ALT, AST, GGT), bilirubin, bile acids, phase 1 and 2 detox markers if possible.
- Systemic: Inflammatory markers (CRP), nutrient levels (zinc, vitamin D, omega-3s, B vitamins).

## Step 2: Dietary Protocols

### 2.1 Anti-inflammatory Gut-Healing Diet

Eliminate: Processed foods, sugar, gluten, dairy (if sensitive), alcohol, fried foods, and food allergens.

Focus on:

- Fiber-rich vegetables (carrots, zucchini, leafy greens).
- Fermented foods (kimchi, sauerkraut, kefir) – if tolerated.
- Healthy fats (olive oil, avocado, omega-3 rich fish).
- Lean protein (organic poultry, fish, plant-based proteins).
- Hydration (water with lemon, herbal teas).

### 2.2 Support Liver Detox Diet

- Include cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower, kale) for phase 2 liver detox enzymes.
- Foods rich in sulfur (onion, garlic).
- Bitter foods to stimulate bile flow (dandelion greens, arugula).
- Avoid excess caffeine, alcohol, and saturated fats.

## Step 3: Gut Repair & Rebalancing

- **Remove:** Identify and remove gut pathogens, dysbiotic bacteria, Candida, or parasites if indicated.
- **Replace:** Digestive support (HCl, pancreatic enzymes, bile salts).
- **Reinoculate:** Probiotics tailored to skin/gut health (Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG, Bifidobacterium longum).
- **Repair:** Gut-healing nutrients such as L-glutamine, zinc carnosine, aloe vera, slippery elm.

## Step 4: Liver Support

Phase 1 & 2 detox support:

- Milk thistle (Silybum marianum) – antioxidant & liver regeneration.
- N-acetyl cysteine (NAC) – supports glutathione production.
- Turmeric (Curcuma longa) – anti-inflammatory and bile stimulation.
- Dandelion root – enhances bile flow and liver detoxification.
- Artichoke extract – supports bile production and cholesterol metabolism.

Bile flow improvement:

- Bitter herbs (gentian, burdock root).
- Digestive bitters before meals.

## Step 5: Skin Support and Anti-Inflammatory Herbs

- Calendula officinalis: Wound healing, anti-inflammatory.
- Chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla): Soothing skin irritations.
- Neem: Antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory for acne.
- Green tea (Camellia sinensis): Antioxidant, reduces sebum production.
- Omega-3 supplements: Fish oil to reduce skin inflammation.

## Step 6: Lifestyle and Environmental Management

- Stress reduction: Mindfulness, yoga, breathing exercises.
- Sleep: Aim for 7-8 hours of quality sleep.
- Skincare: Use gentle, non-toxic products; avoid harsh detergents, fragrances.
- Avoid environmental toxins: Limit exposure to heavy metals, pollution.
- Exercise: Regular moderate exercise to improve circulation and detoxification.

## Step 7: Monitoring and Follow-up

- Track skin condition changes weekly/monthly.
- Adjust gut and liver protocols based on lab markers and symptoms.
- Retest gut microbiome and liver function as needed.
- **Consider referral to dermatologist if skin condition persists or worsens.**
