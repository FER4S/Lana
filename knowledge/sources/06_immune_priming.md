## Immune Modulation Pre-Protocol: Functional Medicine Primer

(for Deep Clinical Complexities)

### SECTION 1: Functional Medicine Lens on Immune Modulation

When preparing patients with immune-related, chronic, or complex conditions for therapeutic
interventions (e.g., detox, GI repair, rebalancing protocols), we must first assess and
appropriately modulate the immune system.

Immune modulation ≠ immune suppression. It is a rebalancing strategy—based on terrain
assessment—to determine whether the immune system needs:

- **Priming** (stimulation, activation, building)
- **Dampening** (modulation, calming, anti-inflammatory support)

### SECTION 2: Immune Priming vs. Immune Dampening

| | Immune Priming | Immune Dampening |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| GOAL | Enhance vigilance, resilience | Reduce hyper-reactivity, regulate overdrive |
| WHEN to use | Frequent infections, low WBC, poor vaccine response, immune "fatigue" | Autoimmunity, histamine overload, MCAS, allergies, PANS/PANDAS |
| KEY LABS | Low IgA/IgG, lymphopenia, low CRP, low cytokines | High ANA, ESR, CRP, eosinophils, IgE, IL-6, TNF-α |
| KEY SYMPTOMS | Frequent colds, slow wound healing, viral reactivations (EBV/CMV) | Joint pain, hives, rashes, fatigue flares, post-viral syndromes |
| FUNCTIONAL SUPPORTS | Mushroom extracts (AHCC, Reishi), Zinc, Vitamin A/D, Astragalus, Colostrum | Quercetin, Curcumin, Omega-3s, LDN, Boswellia, DAO support |
| CAUTION ZONE | Avoid immune boosters if autoimmunity suspected | Avoid dampening if immune exhaustion present |
| MECHANISM | ↑ APC activity, NK cell function, Ig production | ↓ Mast cell activation, cytokine production, immune complexes |

### SECTION 3: Pre-Protocol Terrain Questions

Before starting any protocol (GI, detox, endocrine), ask:

- Is this immune system overreactive, underactive, or dysregulated?
- Is there evidence of stealth infections (Lyme, EBV, mold)?
- Is there a trauma, stress, or vagus nerve component (neuroimmune link)?
- Has the patient had adverse reactions to supplements (e.g. glutathione, probiotics)?
- Is there chronic GI inflammation, leaky gut, or histamine overload?

### SECTION 4: Lab Investigation Guide

Suggested baseline labs (tailor to case complexity):

**Core Immune Terrain:**

- CBC w/ diff (lymphocytes, eosinophils)
- hs-CRP, ESR
- Immunoglobulins (IgA, IgG, IgE, IgM)
- ANA, RF if autoimmune suspected
- Cytokine panel (IL-6, TNF-α) if available

**Adjunctive (based on case):**

- Secretory IgA (stool)
- EBV/CMV/HHV-6 panels (if chronic viral concern)
- Mold/mycotoxin screen (Great Plains or MyMyco)
- DAO, histamine (serum)
- Oxidative stress (8-OHdG), GSH levels
- Stool panel (GI-MAP, GI360)

### SECTION 5: Case Application Flow

1. Assess immune pattern: underactive, overactive, or chaotic?
2. Choose modulation strategy: priming or dampening.
3. Begin pre-protocol support (2–6 weeks depending on severity).
4. Titrate toward target protocol (e.g. gut, detox, hormone).
5. Reassess symptom response and adjust immune support accordingly.

### SECTION 6: Clinical Pearls

- Avoid immune "boosters" in early autoimmune or MCAS cases.
- Always modulate before stimulating detox or mitochondrial protocols in complex patients.
- For post-viral syndromes (e.g. Long COVID, EBV flares): prioritize immune calming, vagus nerve support, mitochondrial repair.

## Use in Complex Cases

Immune Dysregulation, GI Inflammation, Chronic Infections, Autoimmunity, and Metabolic Dysregulation.

### Medicinal Mushrooms

**Key Types:**

- Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum): Adaptogen, immunomodulatory, calming (supports HPA axis).
- Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus): Neuroregenerative (NGF stimulation), brain fog, neuropathy.
- Turkey Tail (Trametes versicolor): Gut microbiome modulation, anti-tumor polysaccharide K (PSK).
- Cordyceps (Cordyceps militaris/sinensis): Mitochondrial function, energy, respiratory endurance.
- Chaga (Inonotus obliquus): Antioxidant, supports inflammatory balance.

**Clinical Applications:**

- Chronic fatigue syndrome, mold illness
- Post-viral syndromes, including post-COVID
- Gut dysbiosis with immunodeficiency
- Mild to moderate cognitive decline

**Formulation Tips:**

- Use dual extraction (alcohol + water) for full-spectrum actives.
- Dose depends on extract potency: often 500–3000 mg/day.
- Best used in rotating protocols or synergistically with adaptogens.

### Bovine Colostrum

**Mechanism:**

- Rich in immunoglobulins (IgG), lactoferrin, growth factors (IGF-1, TGF-β).
- Enhances gut barrier integrity, modulates immune response, and supports mucosal repair.

**Indications:**

- Leaky gut, IBD, IBS, food sensitivities
- Frequent illness, poor mucosal immunity
- Post-antibiotic gut rehabilitation
- Pediatric immune support (use cautiously in CMPA)

**Clinical Pearls:**

- Start low (500–1000 mg daily), titrate to 3 g/day.
- Synergizes with L-glutamine, zinc carnosine, and probiotics.
- Use dairy-free formulations for highly sensitive patients (some forms have casein removed).

**Cautions:**

- Not vegan/vegetarian.
- Avoid in those with cow's milk protein allergy (CMPA).
- Quality and source matter: look for pasture-raised, third-party tested colostrum.

### Serum-Derived Bovine Immunoglobulins (SBI)

**Mechanism:**

- Concentrated immunoglobulins bind microbial antigens (e.g., LPS, exotoxins).
- Reduce gut inflammation and immune overactivation.

**Clinical Applications:**

- SIBO/SIFO with histamine or MCAS overlay
- Post-infectious IBS
- Autoimmune GI (e.g., Crohn's, ulcerative colitis)
- Neurological inflammation from gut-immune axis

**Dosing:**

- Typical: 2.5–5g/day in divided doses.
- Taken with meals or before meals.
- Brands: MegaIgG2000 (Microbiome Labs), IgG Protect (Designs for Health)

**Cautions:**

- Generally well-tolerated, even in dairy-sensitive individuals.
- Can be combined with antimicrobials or binders in GI protocols.

### Related Functional Tools

**A. Lactoferrin** — Antiviral, iron-regulating, anti-biofilm. Used in stealth infections (e.g., Lyme, EBV), dysbiosis.

**B. Beta-glucans (from yeast or mushrooms)** — Immunomodulatory, supports NK cell function. Useful in oncology support, viral reactivation.

**C. Saccharomyces boulardii** — Post-antibiotic microbiota restoration. C. difficile, H. pylori, traveler's diarrhea. Use with caution in SIFO/fungal load.

**D. Pro-resolving mediators (SPMs)** — Specialized omega-3 derivatives. Support resolution phase of inflammation (especially post-infection, autoimmunity).

### Clinical Integration Tips

Always assess for underlying infections, histamine intolerance, and barrier dysfunction before using immune activators.

Use a phased approach:

1. Remove triggers (infections, food antigens)
2. Repair gut and immune resilience (colostrum, immunoglobulins)
3. Rebuild microbiome and neuroimmune tone (mushrooms, pre/probiotics)
4. Rewire with lifestyle, micronutrients, and mind-body work

## Strategies for Preparing and Modulating the Immune System

### Section 1: Immune Priming Foundations

**Goal:** Build terrain resilience before exposure to infections, allergens, stressors, or immune-triggering protocols.

**Optimize Nutrient Status** — Support key nutrients involved in immune cell signaling, barrier integrity, and antioxidant defense.

- Vitamin D3: 40–60 ng/mL target. Involved in immune modulation and antimicrobial peptide production.
- Vitamin A: Essential for mucosal immunity and T-cell function.
- Zinc: Crucial for thymic function and immune cell communication.
- Vitamin C: Enhances neutrophil migration, reduces oxidative stress.
- Selenium: Supports glutathione activity and antiviral defense.
- Iron & B12: Ensure proper white blood cell development and oxygen delivery.

**Regulate Blood Sugar & Insulin** — Hyperglycemia impairs neutrophil and macrophage function.

Strategies:

- Balanced macronutrient meals (protein + fiber + healthy fat)
- Intermittent fasting (if appropriate)
- Limit refined sugars and ultra-processed foods

**Gut Microbiome Support** — 70–80% of immune cells reside in the gut.

Short-term:

- Bone broth, L-glutamine, marshmallow root (mucosal repair)
- Digestive enzymes or bitters for improved protein and micronutrient absorption

Ongoing:

- Probiotic-rich foods (fermented veggies, kefir)
- Prebiotic fiber (onions, leeks, dandelion greens, resistant starches)
- Address dysbiosis (consider stool testing or herbal antimicrobials when needed)

**Mitochondrial & Antioxidant Capacity** — Mitochondria drive energy for immune responses.

- Nutrients: CoQ10, alpha-lipoic acid, NAC, glutathione
- Antioxidant-rich foods: berries, green tea, turmeric, leafy greens
- Remove oxidative stressors: plastics, seed oils, smoking, poor sleep

**Circadian & Sleep Alignment** — Immune cells (like natural killer cells) follow circadian patterns.

- Aim for 7–9 hours of quality sleep
- Prioritize a wind-down routine, darkness at night, AM sunlight
- Melatonin is also an immune modulator

### Section 2: Immune Modulation & Resilience Building

**Goal:** Prevent overactivation (autoimmunity) or underfunction (recurrent infections).

**Immune Tonics** — Gentle, long-term immune-modulating herbs:

- Reishi mushroom: Anti-inflammatory and enhances innate immunity
- Astragalus: Supports white blood cell production and barrier integrity
- Andrographis: Antiviral and modulates cytokine response
- Elderberry (preventive dose only): Enhances cytokine signaling in early infection

**Adaptogens & Stress Buffering** — Chronic stress depletes immune resilience.

- Adaptogens: Ashwagandha, Rhodiola, Holy Basil (rotate as needed)
- Lifestyle: breathwork, daily nature exposure, yoga nidra, therapy when indicated

**Detoxification Pathway Support** — Immune stress increases metabolic waste and cytokine load.

- Daily sweating (sauna, exercise)
- Liver support: cruciferous veggies, dandelion root tea, glycine
- Support bowel movements: magnesium citrate, aloe vera, triphala

### Section 3: Clinical Triggers to Screen For

Important to rule out these root causes before initiating an immune-reset protocol:

- Mold/mycotoxin exposure
- Stealth infections (EBV, Lyme, H. pylori)
- Food sensitivities (gluten, dairy, histamines)
- Leaky gut or SIBO
- Heavy metal burden
- Environmental toxin load (BPA, pesticides)
